Wall Stability & Anti-Sag Test for Travel Adapters.

Wall stability depends on product weight, center of gravity, plug geometry, pin tolerances and the wall socket. This test framework is designed to evaluate how a travel adapter sits, tilts and retains position in representative outlet conditions.

What the Test Evaluates

Initial Fit

Observe insertion feel, seating depth and whether the product sits squarely against the socket.

Tilt / Sag

Observe visible downward rotation or separation from the wall under the product's own weight.

Retention

Evaluate whether the plug remains inserted during normal cable connection and handling.

Cross-Socket Behavior

Compare results across representative new, typical and worn socket conditions where available.

Key Variables to Record

VariableWhy It Matters
Product weightHigher mass increases turning force.
Center-of-gravity distance from wallGreater distance increases leverage.
Body thicknessThicker bodies often move mass farther from the socket.
Plug geometryPin dimensions and tolerances influence retention.
Socket conditionWorn contacts can reduce holding force.
Cable loadingAttached cables can add side or downward force.

Recommended Test Method

  1. Record product weight and body dimensions.
  2. Identify plug mode under test.
  3. Insert into a defined test socket and record seating condition.
  4. Measure or photograph any visible tilt from the wall.
  5. Connect typical charging cables and observe additional movement.
  6. Repeat across multiple socket samples where possible.
  7. Inspect plug pins and mechanism after testing.
Current public data status: LONGRICH has not published a universal anti-sag pass/fail angle or retention-force result yet. The page documents the engineering method only.

Why Flat Architecture Can Help

A flatter product can reduce the distance between the product mass and the wall. This may reduce leverage and improve wall balance, but only when total weight, plug geometry and socket retention are also considered.
