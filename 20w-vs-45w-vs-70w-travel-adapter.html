45W vs 70W: The Buyer Decision

Choose 45W when…

Your users mainly charge phones and tablets, laptop charging is occasional, and the project prioritizes a balanced enclosure, thermal design and target cost.

Choose 70W when…

Laptop charging is a headline benefit, the product needs more single-port headroom, or the brand is targeting a premium multi-device use case.

Choose 20W when…

The product should remain compact and phone-focused, with lower thermal complexity and a value-oriented market position.

MacBook, iPad and iPhone Compatibility

MacBook and USB-C Laptops

Many USB-C MacBook and laptop models can charge from 45W or 70W, but speed depends on the device's input requirement and workload. Check the adapter's single-port USB-C PD output and use a suitable cable.

Read the laptop charging guide →

iPad and Tablets

A compatible iPad or USB-C tablet negotiates the power it accepts. Both 45W and 70W can provide headroom without forcing the full rated output into the device.

iPhone and Smartphones

Compatible phones can fast-charge from USB-C PD while drawing less than the adapter's maximum. Choose the correct cable for the phone model.

Compatibility rule: the largest wattage printed on a multi-port adapter may be its total output, not the output of every port. Verify the designated high-power USB-C port and each multi-device allocation.

Multi-Device Charging: Why 70W Can Matter

When a laptop, iPad and iPhone are connected together, available power is divided according to the adapter's allocation logic. A 70W platform can offer more total headroom, but buyers still need to review the exact output table for the selected port combination.

  • Confirm the maximum output of each USB-C port.
  • Review laptop + phone and laptop + tablet + phone allocations.
  • Check whether connecting a new device briefly triggers power renegotiation.
  • Test the actual device and cable set under realistic load.

Power, Heat and Wall Stability Are One System

Higher-power platforms increase component density and thermal design pressure. A larger or heavier enclosure can also move the center of gravity away from the outlet. Charging power, size, plug layout, heat and wall stability should therefore be evaluated together.

How B2B Buyers Should Specify the Project

  • List the target MacBook, laptop, iPad, phone and other devices.
  • Define whether laptop charging is essential or optional.
  • Set the target retail price and enclosure size.
  • Specify total output, required single-port output and every port combination.
  • Review thermal behavior under realistic full-load conditions.
  • Confirm wall stability, plug locking and the intended certification scope.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 70W always better than 45W?

No. A 45W platform can be smaller, cooler and more cost-effective when premium laptop charging is not the main use case.

Is 45W or 70W better for a MacBook?

It depends on the MacBook model and workload. 45W may suit lighter use; 70W provides more headroom when the device supports it.

Can it charge an iPad and iPhone at the same time?

Yes, with compatible ports and cables, but the available output is divided according to the adapter's multi-port allocation table.

Can 45W charge a laptop?

It can charge selected USB-C laptops. Confirm the laptop's input requirement, supported PD profile, cable and real workload.

Can LONGRICH customize power levels?

Different charging platforms and configurations can be evaluated as part of an OEM or ODM project.
