20W: Best for Compact Travel Products

20W is a strong choice when the target customer mainly charges phones and smaller electronics. It supports a smaller product architecture, easier thermal management and lower cost while still delivering a clear USB-C fast-charging benefit.

45W: The Mainstream Middle Ground

45W provides a broader use case than 20W without the full thermal and size challenge of 70W. It works well for brands targeting business travelers who carry phones, tablets and selected USB-C laptops.

70W: Premium Laptop and Multi-Device Positioning

70W is better suited to premium products where laptop charging is a central selling point. It requires more careful power allocation, thermal design and mechanical packaging, especially when the product also includes multiple international plug mechanisms.

Why More Power Can Create New Mechanical Problems

Higher-power platforms often add component density and thermal structures. That can increase weight or force a larger enclosure, which may affect wall stability. Power, size, plug layout and center of gravity should be designed as one system.

How B2B Buyers Should Choose

  • Start from the target device mix, not the highest possible wattage.
  • Define whether laptop charging is essential or optional.
  • Set target retail price before selecting power level.
  • Consider product thickness and wall-socket stability.
  • Confirm USB-C power allocation when multiple ports are used.
  • Review thermal performance at realistic full-load conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 70W always better than 45W?

No. 70W adds cost, thermal and packaging complexity. 45W may be a better commercial fit if laptop charging is not the main use case.

Is 20W enough for travel?

For phone-focused travelers, often yes. It depends on the devices carried and the desired charging speed.

Can 45W charge a laptop?

It can charge selected USB-C laptops, but actual compatibility depends on the laptop's power requirement.

Can LONGRICH customize power levels?

Different charging platforms and configurations can be evaluated as part of OEM or ODM development.
